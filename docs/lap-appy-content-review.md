# Appendectomy content review

Reviewed September 20, 2026. Scope: Overview and Anatomy. Quiz, Pimp and Steps are flagged here without changing their behavior or content.

## Open question: retrocecal prevalence

The existing Overview states approximately 30%. The original Anatomy page also stated 30%. Quiz question q2 and Pimp question p3 state approximately 65%. Neither claim has a source in the repository.

Do not substitute one percentage for the other without choosing a reference and its population. A 2023 cadaveric study found 23 retrocecal appendices among 47 specimens (48.9%); that is a study-specific result, not a replacement universal prevalence. Source: [Reappraisal of the variational anatomy of the vermiform appendix](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37691509/).

The new Anatomy note describes the position without assigning a percentage. The Overview's existing numeric claim and the Quiz/Pimp claims are retained pending editorial review. Decide whether to remove numeric prevalence throughout the module or explicitly teach a sourced textbook estimate with appropriate context. Do not treat the existing quiz answer as settled by this visual update.

## Anatomy wording changes

- Describe cecal mobilization as potentially needed rather than universally required. The Overview already used conditional wording; Steps still says it must be done.
- Describe the appendiceal artery as arising from the ileocolic arterial system, acknowledging branching variation.
- Focus the terminal ileum note on identification rather than making a categorical recommendation about exploring for Meckel's diverticulum.
- Replace the checklist's “ready to scrub” declaration with “Identification review complete.” The checklist is a study aid, not a clinical competency assessment.
- Omit a fixed appendix length from the identification note; it is unnecessary to the interaction and reported normal lengths vary.

Reference: [StatPearls: Appendectomy, updated May 14, 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK580514/?report=printable), anatomy and laparoscopic technique sections. This supports the taenia convergence landmark, the mesoappendix relationship, arterial variation, and conditional mobilization for a retrocecal appendix.

## Additional editorial question

The Overview lists interval appendectomy after nonoperative management without specifying patient selection. This should be reviewed against the clinical guideline and patient population the course intends to teach. No treatment-policy change is made in this design update.
